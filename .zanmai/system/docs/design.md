# Designing documents

Flyers, one-pagers and slide decks built in your organisation's own visual language.

## What Zanmai needs from you

The brand cannot be guessed, so it is read from real material. Have ready at least one existing piece in the look you want, a flyer, a deck, a template file, plus the logo and any imagery that should be used. Without a template Zanmai asks for one instead of inventing a house style.

Three things are settled with you before it starts: who the piece is for, what it should achieve, and whether you want a **copy** of an existing template with new content or a **new piece** composed in the same visual language. Copy is for "same as this one"; composing is the default for "make me a flyer about X".

## How the piece is built

Your templates are read for their concrete values: the exact colours, type sizes, spacing rhythm, block geometry and recurring motifs. Those become a reusable kit for that brand and format, which is why a second piece in the same style comes faster than the first.

The structure then follows your content rather than the template's boxes. Several things compared on the same attributes become a table, repeated items of the same shape become a grid whose count comes from the number of items, a sequence becomes a flow. That is the difference between a designed piece and a template with text dropped into it, where blocks end up two-thirds empty.

While building, Zanmai renders the piece and looks at the result instead of trusting its own description of it, then fixes what it sees. It checks the measurable things itself: nothing overflowing or colliding, no empty block, contrast sufficient, the brand's real values used. The taste call it does not make for you. You get a preview and decide.

## What you get

By default a rendered page: a file for the screen, or a print-ready PDF with the fonts embedded and verified rather than merely referenced. Page size comes from context, A4 in Europe, Letter in the US, or whatever the piece calls for. Everything lands in `_export`, one folder per piece, ready to pull out.

When you need a file you can keep editing yourself, the piece is built natively in Affinity or PowerPoint instead. Say so up front, because it changes how it is made.

Two limits are named rather than worked around. A real press run with an exact colour profile, bleed and crop marks belongs in a native tool; a CMYK copy can be produced, but this is not a prepress step. And the screen render needs a Chromium-based browser, which is checked before the job starts rather than halfway through.

## What never happens

Your originals are not touched; Zanmai works on copies and writes new files. Colours and fonts come from your material, never from memory or approximation. Content is never quietly dropped to make a layout problem disappear, and if something does not fit you are told. No image is generated behind your back either: missing imagery is raised with you first, together with the cost, because generating spends money and commits the piece to AI imagery.
