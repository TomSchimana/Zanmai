# Editing video

**Read this when:** footage has to become a finished cut.

Raw footage and a rough idea in, a finished cut out: trimmed, levelled, captioned, and in the format you need it for.

## What you hand over

Where the footage is, and where your script or your notes are. Notes matter more than they sound: they carry the direction, which music, "start close and pull out", which graphic on which line, links to references. Whatever they point at gets found rather than handed over separately.

Then: what the video is for and who watches it, which format or formats you want, and what you explicitly do not want. If no brand is set up yet, the job stops there, because captions, a logo and any graphic need colours, a typeface and a mark ([your brand](brand.md)).

## You see the plan before the work

Transcribing is cheap and it gives the editor the content, so you get a proposal in plain words first: how long the piece is and what character it has, how tight the cut should be as an actual number, what treatment is recommended and what would be left alone, and for every place something would be added, what kind and why. Where footage would be generated, the shot is described before it exists, with what it costs.

You read that and say go, or change it. Nobody should be surprised by a finished render.

**Restraint is the default.** Most jobs are a plain cut: trim, tighten, level the sound, done. A job that asks for captions gets captions and nothing else. An insert has to earn its place by helping you understand something, never by showing what the tool can do.

## How the cut is decided

By reading, not by watching. The transcript carries word-level timing, so silences, false starts, filler and tangents can be found in the text, and where a line was recorded twice the last take wins. You get the decision as a cut sheet you can read and check without opening the video.

Where a removed pause leaves a visible jump, it gets covered: a second camera where one exists, otherwise a slight size change, otherwise material that belongs there anyway. Only where it actually shows.

## Editing by editing the text

The transcript comes back as an ordinary text file. Delete a sentence or a paragraph, hand it back, and exactly that leaves the video. Correcting a word does something different: it changes the captions, not the cut. So the whole edit is readable before it exists, and you never have to describe a timecode.

## Format, captions, sound

The target format is settled before captions and graphics, because it decides safe areas, type size and how the frame is divided. Two formats share the same rough cut and split from there, so the expensive part runs once. Every reframe is checked against what got cropped out: a face, a caption, the thing the sentence is about.

Short pieces get word-by-word captions, long ones a subtitle track the player draws, which stays readable at any size. The sound is carried uncompressed between stages and levelled exactly once at the end.

## It watches its own render

Two passes before you see anything: the technical one as a checklist, then composition as its own separate look, because a checklist walks straight past "why is that at the top". At most two rounds; whatever two rounds did not fix comes back to you named as an open point rather than disappearing into another round.

## What you get

One file per purpose. The master is never overwritten, smaller versions sit beside it with the purpose in the name. The transcript, the cut sheet and the graphics source stay with the job, so it can be reopened later instead of started again.
