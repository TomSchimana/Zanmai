# The idea behind Zanmai

**Read this when:** the question is why the space is cut this way.

Zanmai sorts by what happens to something next, not by what it is about. That one decision explains most of what follows.

## What it is for

You put things down here so you do not have to hold on to them, and what is left over is attention:
for the decision, the idea, the thing you actually wanted to do.

That is where the name comes from. Zanmai (三昧) is the Japanese word for being completely taken up
by one thing, samadhi in Buddhism, and in ordinary speech an ending you hang on a word to say
nothing but that. It only happens when the other twenty things are not pulling at you.

## Remembering is only half of it

This is the part almost every system gets wrong, and most assistants along with them. A brain is not
a warehouse. It connects things, notices patterns, and makes something that was not there before.

So Zanmai helps on both sides. It takes the weight of remembering, and it works with what it holds:
a decision you can now actually make, a piece of research, a document, an image. The pile is not the
product. What you do with it is.

It is also the reason nothing is ever quietly deleted. What you forget is exactly what the space is
for. Something you have not touched in a year is not rubbish, it is out of sight: still there, still
findable, just no longer in the way.

## A workplace, not a filing plan

Most systems sort by importance or by stage: what matters, what is current, what is done. That goes
wrong for a reason that has nothing to do with discipline. Importance changes without anyone
noticing. The trip is cancelled and the folder still says you are working on it, because nothing is
broken and nobody has a reason to tidy. A month later you cannot tell from the outside which of
twenty folders still holds.

So the question is a different one, and you can answer it without having to judge yourself: **what
happens to this next.**

Think of how you work at a desk. Things arrive somewhere. You take one of them, put it in front of
you and work on it, and when it is done you put it away: into your own affairs, into what you know,
or into the filing cabinet. Some of that has to be cleared regularly, some of it grows for years,
and you know which is which without thinking about it. That is the whole structure, and it holds
because it describes what you do rather than what you feel about a thing.

## Nothing has to travel

There is no route you owe anybody. What is clear from the outset goes straight where it belongs: you
take the invoice out of the letterbox, see what it is, and file it. It lands on the desk only if you
have to pay it first.

Only the desk gets cleared. Everywhere else, staying put is the normal case rather than a backlog.
Something nobody ever looks at again has still ended up in the right place.

## Connected, not filed away

Everything is linked. A subject, a person, a company, an invoice, a policy. So an invoice is not a
lonely PDF in a folder, it is attached to the insurance it belongs to, which is attached to the
company, which is attached to the person you talked to.

That is what lets you ask a question in your own words and get an answer, instead of trying to
remember which folder you chose two years ago. It is also why a growing space gets more useful
rather than heavier. Hundreds of documents work. So do thousands.

## What stays out of the way

Nothing you write is rewritten. Structure happens around your words, never inside them. The folders
you make are yours; none of them ships with the space. And what runs the machine sits in one place
you never have to open.

## In practice

You collect things as they come up. You write a journal. You have things researched. Over months
that turns into a body of knowledge that is yours, connected and current.

Then it pays off twice. You make decisions on top of it, and Zanmai helps you think them through.
And when something has to come out of it, a document, a piece of research, an image, it gets built
from the material you already gathered instead of from nothing.
