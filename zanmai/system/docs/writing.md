# Documents written for you

Minutes from a meeting recording, a write-up of a video, an overview of material you already have, a handover, a letter. Anything longer than a line that is not filing, a design piece or a research report is written here.

## From a recording to the minutes

Put the recording in `import/`, or point at a file wherever it sits. It is transcribed on your own machine and read against your vault, so the names in it come out the way you write them; nothing about that step is uploaded, and the recording itself is kept. [Speaking instead of typing](voice.md) covers that side in full, and it is the same path whether the file is a spoken note or two hours of a meeting.

The write-up is then made from the transcript, not from the recording being listened to a second time. What was actually said is the source, and nothing outside it goes in.

A video is read on both tracks, the spoken words and the picture, which is why a number that was only ever on a slide still lands in the text. [Research](research.md) describes how that reading works.

## The one line before anything is written

You get a single sentence naming what will be written, plus at most three bullets where a real choice was made. You answer go, or you change one word in it.

That is the whole gate, and it is deliberately not a form. Four things have to be settled before a first sentence exists, and they are answered from the material and from your vault rather than asked back at you:

- **The source.** What counts as the material, and what is out of scope. Nothing from general knowledge leaks in as though it came from your recording.
- **What it is for, and who reads it.** Including who appears in it by name.
- **The frame.** Length, how people are addressed, and what must not appear.
- **The format.** Where it lands and in what shape, so you are not rebuilding it by hand afterwards.

Where your vault already holds a document of the same kind, that one sets the structure and the voice. A house style you already have beats a fresh guess every time, and the proposal says which document it is shaped like.

## When other people are in it

A summary of a meeting is read by the people who were in it, and it stays in the vault. So an unbriefed write-up is the risk, not the effort.

What that means in practice: people said, described, asked and proposed things; they did not insist, concede or hold their ground, because what was observed is that a sentence was spoken. There are no rankings nobody asked for, no sharpest point and no turning point. Opinions are not filtered out, they are attributed, as a quote with the name behind it or as one person's view stated plainly. Timestamps per statement are only written if you asked for them.

## Where it ends up

In `doing/`, one folder per piece, with the drafts beside the finished file. You get a short summary in the reply, the path, and an offer to open it. It only opens if you say yes.

A short piece is written in the conversation. Anything that takes minutes runs in the background so you can carry on, and you are told in one line that it is running.

## Asking for one

`/zanmai-write` asks directly. You rarely need it: a document is written whenever a document is what you asked for.
